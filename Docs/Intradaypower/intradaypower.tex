\section{Introduction}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Commodity curves in ORE return a single price per delivery date. This is sufficient for products that settle against a
daily price, but power markets trade at a finer granularity. Intraday products reference prices for specific parts of
the day, e.g.\ the 09:00--10:00 hour on a weekday.

The earlier approach modelled this with dedicated load curves, e.g.\ peak and off-peak curves built from the
respective futures. A separate curve was needed for every delivery pattern. This was cumbersome to configure, made
historical fixings hard to manage, and offered no support for intraday fixings within the delivery day.

The intraday power framework replaces these dedicated curves with a single curve per underlying that carries an
intraday shape, combined with a trade-specific load profile. This supports flexible and overlapping delivery windows
without a new curve per pattern. The scope of this first version is limited to intraday power forwards and swaps;
exposure simulation of these products is not yet covered.

A day-level price comes from a commodity price curve. Intraday shape factors, provided as market data, scale this
price across the buckets of the day. A trade specifies which buckets it delivers into through its load profile. The
pricing of overlapping intervals and the treatment of daylight saving are described in Section
\ref{ss:intraday_power_pricing}.

This is the first version of intraday power support in ORE. Some configuration and parameterisation details may change
as the feature set grows.

\section{Curve Configuration and Market Data}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An intraday power curve has two components. A commodity price curve provides the day-level price, and a
set of intraday shape factors distributes this price across the buckets of the day. The shape factors are market data
and are loaded through the curve configuration.

The shape resolution is flexible. A single curve can mix buckets of different lengths, e.g.\ 15 minute buckets for the
next day, hourly buckets further out and daily buckets beyond that. Alternatively, separate curves can be set up per
granularity for the same underlying, e.g.\ a 15 minute curve and an hourly curve. Separate curves are reported on
different risk factor keys, so their stress and sensitivity results needs to be aggregated or simulated through curve algebra (see the curve algebra section of the User Guide).

\section{Historical Fixings}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The load profile of a trade determines the granularity of the historical fixings. An hourly profile expects hourly
fixings, a 15 minute profile expects 15 minute fixings.

Fixing names are derived from the underlying name, the delivery date and the delivery window. The day-level name has
the form \lstinline!POWER-<UnderlyingName>-<DeliveryDate>!, where the delivery date is an ISO date, e.g.\
\lstinline!POWER-PJM_WH_RT_PK-2021-06-01!. For an intraday bucket the start and end of the window in seconds from
midnight are appended, e.g.\ \lstinline!POWER-PJM_WH_RT_PK-2021-06-01-32400-36000! for the 09:00--10:00 hour
($9 \times 3600 = 32400$, $10 \times 3600 = 36000$). The extra hour created when clocks move back at the end of
daylight saving carries an additional \lstinline!-DST! suffix. A bucket that covers the full day (0 to 86400 seconds)
uses the plain day-level name.

For a trade with a load profile, one fixing is expected per bucket with positive load. The index fixing for the
delivery date is the load-weighted average of the bucket fixings. Without a load profile, a single full-day fixing
under the day-level name is used.

Intraday fixings on the delivery date itself are supported. On the evaluation date, buckets whose fixing has already
been published are taken from the history, and buckets without a published fixing are forecast from the curve. For an
hourly profile delivering from 09:00 to 13:00, observed at 11:00 with fixings published for the 09:00 and 10:00
buckets, those two fixings are used and the 11:00 and 12:00 buckets are forecast. For dates strictly before the
evaluation date all expected bucket fixings must be present.
