\subsubsection{Digital FX Option}
\label{pricing:fx_digitaloption}

Digital FX Options are cash or nothing instruments on an underlying FX rate.
They are always cash settled, allowing the cash payment date to be later than
the option expiry date. They can be either priced
\begin{itemize}
\item analytically using formulas developed by \cite{Rubinstein_1991} 
based on the Black-Scholes framework, see \cite{Haug_1997} Ch 4.19.2, or
\item as call respectively put spreads
\end{itemize}

Both pricing approaches are available for the trade type \lstinline!FxDigitalOption!
and are selected via the engine configuration:
\begin{itemize}
\item engine \lstinline!AnalyticCashSettledEuropeanEngine!: analytic pricing as
described in section \ref{pricing:fx_option_digital_analytical} (default),
\item engine \lstinline!CallSpreadEngine!: call/put spread replication as described
in section \ref{pricing:fx_option_digital_callspread}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Analytical Digital FX Option}\label{pricing:fx_option_digital_analytical}

The NPV of a Digital FX option can be expressed as:
$$
\NPV = N \cdot P(t_p)\cdot \Phi(\omega\,d), \qquad d = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}\left(\ln\frac{F_T}{K} - \frac{\sigma^2 T}{2}\right)
$$
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N$: the amount to be paid at the payment date if the option is in-the-money
\item $P(t_p)$: the discount factor for the cash payment date $t_p$
\item $\Phi(\cdot)$: the cumulative standard normal distribution
\item $\omega$: 1 for a call option, -1 for a put option
\item $F_T$: the forward projected FX rate for the expiry time $T$ at time 0  
\item $K$: the strike FX rate
\item $\sigma$: the volatility FX forward rate
\end{itemize}

If the option has already expired but the cash payment has not yet been made
($t \geq T$, $t < t_p$), the payoff is fixed and is simply discounted from the
payment date $t_p$.

\subsubsection*{Digital FX Option as Call/Put Spread}\label{pricing:fx_option_digital_callspread}

Alternatively, when a more accurate reflection of volatility smiles is a priority, 
Digital FX options can be priced using call/put spreads (i.e. by approximating 
the digital FX option payment with the difference between two European vanilla 
FX options). Given a digital call/put option with strike rate K, the two
vanilla call/put options will have strikes $K_- \leq K$ and $K_+\geq K$, where 
$K_\pm=K \pm \epsilon/2$ and $\epsilon$ is a small spread width centred on $K$.

The present value of digital FX call and put options with strike K and payout 
amount $M$ in currency A, can be expressed in terms of bought and sold vanilla 
FX calls/puts to exchange amount $N$ of currency B for amount $K_\pm \cdot N$ 
in currency A. All PVs are expressed in currency A:
\begin{align*}
\NPV_{DigitalCall}(M,K) &\approx\NPV_{Call}(N,K_-) - \NPV_{Call}(N,K_+) \\
\NPV_{DigitalPut}(M,K) &\approx\NPV_{Put}(N,K_+) - \NPV_{Put}(N,K_-) 
\end{align*}
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $M$: the amount in currency A to be paid at expiration if the digital 
call/put is in-the-money
\item $N = M/(K_+ - K_-) = M/\epsilon$
\end{itemize}

The spread width $\epsilon$ is configurable via the engine parameter
\lstinline!CallSpreadEps! (default $10^{-4}$). The European vanilla FX options
are priced with the Garman-Kohlhagen / Black76 analytic formula (Section
\ref{pricing:fx_option}), evaluated at strikes $K_\pm$ so that the market
volatility smile is naturally incorporated.

As for the analytic engine, the digital is cash settled: the call/put spread is
priced assuming payment on the option expiry date $T$ and the deferred payment to
$t_p$ is then accounted for by multiplying with the forward discount factor
$P(T,t_p)$ between expiry and payment date. If the option has already expired
($t \geq T$), the payoff is fixed and is discounted from the payment date $t_p$.
