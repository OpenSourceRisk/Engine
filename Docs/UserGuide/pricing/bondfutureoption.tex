\subsubsection{European Bond Future Option}
\label{pricing:european-bond-future-option}

Vanilla European bond future options are priced analytically using the Black-76 
model and log-normal volatilities.

The Black-76 model assumes that the futures price process, $F(t),\; t \in [0,T]$, for a 
futures contract maturing at $T$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
dF(t) = \sigma F(t) \, dW(t)
\label{eq:black76-bond-future-price-process}
\end{equation}
where $W(t)$ is a standard Brownian motion under the risk-neutral measure.

This leads to a Black-76 analytical solution for the present value of a European 
bond future option, given by:
\begin{equation*}
\Pi = N P(T) \operatorname{Black}(K, F(0), \nu, \omega)
\end{equation*}
where $\nu = \sigma \sqrt{T}$ and:
\begin{itemize}
\item N: bond future option contract notional
\item $K$: strike price
\item $F(0)$: the futures contract price at time 0 i.e.\ on the valuation date
\item $\sigma$: the Black volatility of the futures price process
\item $\omega$: $1$ for a call option and $-1$ for a put option
\item $P(T)$: the discount factor for maturity time $T$
\end{itemize}

See Black Model, Section~\ref{models:black}.

\subsubsection{American Bond Future Option}
\label{pricing:american-bond-future-option}

Vanilla American bond future options can be priced approximately using the Barone-Adesi 
and Whaley model with a single volatility applicable for the trade's expiry and strike.
See Section~\ref{models:barone-adesi} for additional details. Note that in that section, 
$r_d - r_f = 0$, since we have the futures price process given in 
Equation~\eqref{eq:black76-bond-future-price-process}. Also, in that section, the 
European price is calculated as outlined in Section~\ref{pricing:european-bond-future-option}.

Vanilla American bond future options can also be priced using Finite Difference Methods 
within the Black-Scholes framework using a time-dependent volatility. See 
Section~\ref{pricing:fx_americanoption} for a brief summary of the options.

Note that in market practice, American bond futures options are often quoted and risk-managed 
using European Black-76 implied volatilities. This approximation is generally reasonable for 
liquid exchange-traded bond futures options, where option maturities are typically short and 
the value of the early exercise feature is often small. For this reason, we allow bond 
future option trades and bond future volatility curve configurations to specify American 
exercise but also provide a flag to allow the trades to be priced and option volatilities 
to be stripped assuming European exercise and the European pricing model.
